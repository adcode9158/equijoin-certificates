# Membership and execution experiments (protocol excerpt)

We will test two claims separately. The paper proves how many point answers suffice on a known instance. A deployment claim additionally requires that a service can use available information to choose an inexpensive execution or resource-allocation path. Faster replay of an offline minimum alone cannot validate that claim.

1. Use the Spider 1.0 development SQL and its local SQLite databases as a new corpus for this join-certificate study. Retain the previous budget grid 10, 100, 1,000, 10,000, 100,000, with 100 primary. Count distinct normalized filtered two-table inner-join intermediates, preserve all WHERE predicates, and cross-check actual cardinalities against SQL. Report the original BIRD parser's narrow cohort separately from a prespecified schema-aware extension allowing unambiguous unqualified columns and unique non-null single-column declared primary keys of other types. Treat an intermediate repeated by questions with different final projections as one observation. Record parsing, execution and input availability issues, including discrepancies with any key-affinity assumptions. The databases are academic examples and may be too small to exercise the decision boundary.
2. For every fitting intermediate, compute the minimum with the exact passing-row count, one extra allowed row, and no count, holding the filtered fact counts fixed. Separately, on every eligible singleton-predicate instance at budget 100 in Spider and the previous BIRD cohorts, compute how the minimum for cap two varies over all possible choices of the sole passing key whose join still fits. Record the extremes and the actual position; uniformly counting keys describes alternate assignments, not a production frequency.
3. For the paper's BIRD badge example, check an alternative assignment attaining the cheapest certificate by re-evaluating the filter and join on row data. Use only a swap of the two display names, preserving every user identifier, every fact row and the multiset of names. Compare the numerical certificate to a second algorithm and to enumerating all worlds on tiny inputs. This tests whether the optimum reported for the observed database can be picked from the available catalog alone.
4. Complement these falsification attempts with prospective, charged physical-operator choices and an independent foundations/prior-art audit. A lack of power in Spider will be reported as such.
